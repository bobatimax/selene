Analysis of Permanently Shadowed Regions (PSRs) near the lunar south pole that act as cryogenic cold traps, preserving water ice and trapped volatiles over geological timescales.

Scientific Evidence & Verification

  • Orbital Instruments: NASA LRO Diviner thermal radiometer (K) and LOLA laser altimetry (m).
  • Remote Signatures: LEND neutron spectrometer hydrogen suppression and LAMP ultraviolet albedo anomalies.
  • Confidence Level: High (Validated by NASA LCROSS impact plume & KASA ShadowCam).
  • Scientific Caveat: Permanent shadow confirms thermal stability for ice, but local topographic roughness and overburden require subsurface drilling to verify extractable yield.

Lunar South Pole Mission Planner

Explore • Compare • Plan • Learn

In a Nutshell

An interactive 3D website that lets you explore the Moon’s south pole, compare real landing sites, and plan future missions — all using public NASA data.

🎯

How It Helps

  • Makes lunar science visual and easy to understand
  • Compare real Artemis candidate sites side-by-side
  • See sunlight, radio links, terrain, and ice potential
  • Practice mission and rover planning
  • Works online with live JPL data and offline too
✨

Why the South Pole?

Some ridges get almost continuous sunlight for power, while nearby permanently shadowed craters stay cold enough to trap water ice — a rare and valuable combination for future exploration.
